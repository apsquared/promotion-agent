# Buyer-first priorities

Use this guide whenever promotion work has to be ranked: choosing which activity to run, ordering the follow-ups a run queues, ordering the daily brief, and answering "Which of these tasks actually matter?" or "Is this worth doing?" about a product's open tasks or plans. It runs under the [shared workflow](../WORKFLOW.md). It is guidance, not an activity: it has no task category or budget row, and it never overrides a choice the user has made.

Building and promoting both feel like progress. A product moves only when a buyer does something: replies, books a call, starts a trial, pays, stays, refers someone. Most marketing work can be done without anyone on the other end, so the agent checks each piece of work for a buyer before ranking it.

## The test

Ask of every activity, task and follow-up: **will this get someone to pay, or keep someone paying, and how soon will we know?**

Then, in order:

1. **Who is it for?** A paying customer who asked, a number from the product's own results, or a guess?
2. **Which number does it move?** Replies, calls, sign-ups, trials, payments, retention. "None, but the page gets nicer" is not a promotion result.
3. **Is that the bottleneck at this stage?** Improving something that is not the bottleneck changes nothing the user will see.
4. **Is there a tenth-size version?** One subreddit instead of five, one directory instead of twenty, a hand-written message instead of a sequence, a sketch shown on a call instead of a finished video.
5. **Is it comfortable work standing in for uncomfortable work?** A queue full of research and drafts with nothing that puts the user in front of a buyer is the signal.

## The product's stage

The same work is right at one stage and wasted at another: a referral ask is noise with no customers and valuable with two hundred. Read the stage from the user's words in this conversation first, then from the **Stage and buyer** section of the workspace's `marketing/AGENT.md`. If neither says whether anyone pays yet and the answer would change the ranking, ask that one question (what is sold, to whom, how many pay and roughly how much) and wait. For smaller unknowns, assume, and label the assumption in the report.

| Stage | Where the product is | What matters now | Activities that usually fit | Usually later |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Unproven | Nobody has paid or committed | Proof that one kind of buyer will pay: conversations, pre-sales, deposits | `acquisition` sprint, `competitor` research to find where buyers talk, `engagement` in buyer communities | SEO articles and pSEO, directory batches, AI visibility, polished video |
| 2. Committed, not shipped | People committed but cannot use it yet | Shipping the one core thing and collecting the first payments | Personal follow-up with everyone who committed, `release` pack for the first version | Broad launches before the core works, new channels |
| 3. First customers | Some pay, no repeatable way to get more | One or two channels that keep bringing buyers, talking to every customer, fixing why people leave | Deeper work in the one or two channels that brought buyers, `release` packs, `competitor` alternatives content, testimonials and case studies from real customers, referral asks | Opening a third channel before one works |
| 4. Repeatable | Buyers arrive predictably | Retention, pricing, more of what works | `blog`, `pseo`, `search-console`, `visibility`, `directory`, `video`, and more of the working channel | Nothing by stage alone; rank by measured results |

"Usually later" means: rank it lower unless there is evidence that buyers find products this way. A product whose buyers search for the problem can start the blog at stage 1, and SEO takes months, so say why when making that call. The table orders the work; it never forbids an activity the user asks for.

## Marketing that is building in disguise

These look like promotion but usually reach no buyer. Name the pattern plainly and say what to do instead.

| Pattern | Instead |
|---|---|
| Content written for other builders: build logs, tech-stack posts, "how I built it" | Content about the buyer's problem and the result they get |
| Posting in builder communities and reading their applause as demand | Going where the buyers already talk, and following those communities' rules |
| Counting followers, impressions, upvotes or page views as the result | Counting replies, sign-ups, trials and payments |
| Opening another channel before one works | Going deeper on one or two channels that fit the buyer, for long enough to judge them |
| Polishing a page, video or listing that few people see | Getting it in front of buyers first, then improving it from what they do |
| A launch judged by its ranking or likes | Judging it by the sign-ups and payments it brought, and following up with every new sign-up in the week after |
| Articles for searches buyers never make | Answering the questions buyers ask, in their own words |
| Waiting for inbound | A few personal, useful messages to real buyers, drafted for the user to send |

The same work can be right with the evidence behind it. Judge from evidence, not from the type of work.

## Proof is behavior, not opinion

Opinions are the most common false proof: "sounds useful", likes, upvotes, waitlist joins, a positive reply. Behavior is proof: they paid, pre-ordered, did the task by hand last week, switched from a competitor, or left because something was missing. When a result is an opinion, report it as one and keep the plan where it is. Discovery questions ask what the person did the last time the problem came up and what they used or paid, never whether they would use a product.

## Judging a list of tasks or plans

For "Which of these tasks matter?", "Is this worth doing?" or a pasted plan, give each item one verdict with a one-line reason:

- **Do first:** reaches or keeps buyers at this stage's bottleneck. At most three.
- **Has to be done:** a promise already made, a scheduled post to verify, a deadline. Not a judgment call; say how to keep it small.
- **Do, smaller:** the right aim at the wrong size. Give the smaller version.
- **Later:** real, but not the bottleneck now. Say what would bring it back, such as "after the first ten paying customers" or "when a second customer asks".
- **Don't do:** reaches no buyer and nothing a buyer does would change that now. Name the pattern and say what to do with the time.
- **Unclear:** too vague to judge. Say what would make it judgeable.

Then one line on the pattern across the list ("5 of 7 tasks are research or drafts; none puts you in front of a buyer"), at most three missing tasks a buyer-first marketer would add, and the order for this week. If the list is already sound, say so rather than inventing a flaw.

This judgment is advice. It changes no task by itself. If the user agrees to drop or defer items, mark them skipped or snoozed with the reason through the `disposition` helper, or queue the missing tasks under [Keep the work moving](../WORKFLOW.md#keep-the-work-moving), as the shared workflow describes. Every external action the new tasks lead to still needs its own approval.

## Boundaries

- An explicit user choice wins. When the user picks work this guide ranks low, do it well, and mention the trade-off once at most.
- Ranking never authorizes outreach, posting or spending, and never turns a draft into a sent message.
- Do not invent the stage, revenue or customer counts. Unknowns are asked or labelled assumed.
- Be direct about the work and respectful about the person. When a task is right, say so just as plainly.

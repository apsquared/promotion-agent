# Competitor analysis and mention discovery

Use activity `competitor` under the [shared workflow](../WORKFLOW.md). The outcome is a sourced comparison plus a practical shortlist of places where competitors are being discussed. This is a research activity in the current conversation, not a recurring monitor or permission to contact anyone.

## Choose the comparison set

Use competitors named by the user or recorded in the selected product's marketing context. Otherwise derive a small candidate set from the product's actual audience and problem, then verify the candidates against their official websites. Default to 3–5 competitors; narrow the set for a focused request. Explain why each overlaps and distinguish direct competitors from adjacent tools and substitutes. Resolve ambiguous names with domains, handles, or product context before counting mentions.

Read the selected product's facts and existing competitor reports, tasks, and thread/link history. Do not scan other local product repositories. Missing competitor configuration does not block research when the audience and product purpose are known. Ask only for essential unknowns; label other uncertainties.

## Compare what can be verified

For each competitor, record its official URL, audience/use case, positioning, and relevant capabilities or limitations. Include pricing only when it matters to the user's question and can be verified from a current official source. Capture source URLs and the date checked. Attribute vendor claims and keep your interpretation separate from documented facts. Compare against sourced facts about the user's product; do not invent superiority, market share, revenue, adoption, or missing features from silence in documentation.

Use the comparison to decide what conversations are relevant: alternatives, switching, recommendations, frustrations, questions about a feature, or successful use cases. Include positive and neutral discussions, not only complaints.

## Find where competitors are discussed

Use the current session's web search, browser, or available connectors. Search distinctive product names, domains, and known aliases alongside relevant use-case terms. Combine broad web discovery with platform-specific searches where useful: Reddit, X, Hacker News, public forums, review sites, videos/comments, newsletters, and comparison articles. Choose sources that fit the audience; do not mechanically search every platform. Specialized search skills can help if available, but are not required dependencies.

Honor the user's date range. Otherwise start with the last 30 days for engagement opportunities and separate useful older/evergreen sources. Record what was searched and when. Search-engine date filters and snippets are discovery aids, not proof of publication dates or actual mentions.

Open each shortlisted source and verify that it refers to the correct competitor. Prefer the original post or review over reposts and aggregators. Keep the exact post/comment URL where possible. Record:

- Competitor, platform/community, source URL, and discussion title/topic.
- Publication/activity date when visible, and date checked; label unavailable dates unknown.
- A short evidence excerpt or concise paraphrase of the actual mention and its context.
- Discussion type: recommendation, comparison, complaint, question, praise, or another grounded description.
- Source relationship: independent discussion/review, vendor-owned promotion, sponsored/affiliate content, or unknown. Explain visible evidence for paid placement rather than guessing.
- Product relevance, whether a helpful contribution is possible, and any visible barrier such as a locked thread or community promotion rule.

Deduplicate by canonical discussion URL and competitor identity, including prior reports, open/done engagement tasks, and skipped/snoozed dispositions. Several comments in the same thread are one opportunity unless they warrant separate treatment. Revisit a previously recorded discussion only for a meaningful new development, and explain what changed.

Default to 5–10 verified mentions across the selected competitors, not a minimum quota. Report fewer when that is what the evidence supports. Put inaccessible pages and snippet-only leads in a separate unverified section; never invent contents, dates, engagement metrics, or a successful search. If live research tools are unavailable, provide a clearly labeled research plan and known facts, not a claimed mention report. No results means none were verified in this search, not that the competitor has no mentions.

## Turn evidence into useful next steps

Rank opportunities by audience fit, specificity of need, recency/activity, and whether a contribution would add value. A competitor mention or complaint is not confirmed buying intent. Do not infer trend frequency, market sentiment, or channel performance from a small search sample. Distinguish observed evidence from hypotheses about positioning or content opportunities.

Recommend an appropriate next step for each strong finding: helpful reply, watch for updates, content idea, positioning lesson, or skip. "Watch" is a recommendation only; do not create an automation unless asked. For up to 2–3 suitable conversations, include a concise draft reply with its exact destination and rationale. Avoid pretending to be a customer or independent reviewer. Disclose affiliation when relevant and follow the community's rules. Prepare drafts only; the shared workflow governs any separately authorized action.

## Save a reviewable report

Write a dated Markdown report in an existing permitted draft/research location. Use `marketing/drafts/competitor-analysis-YYYY-MM-DD.md` only when that path is permitted, and preserve prior reports by choosing a new revision filename if needed. If no local write location is authorized, return the report in the conversation. Include:

1. Scope: product, selected competitors, research window, search coverage, and limitations.
2. A sourced competitor comparison, with factual differences and explicitly labeled interpretations.
3. Verified mention table and a separate list of unverified leads, if any.
4. Prioritized opportunities, optional reply drafts, and concrete suggested next actions.
5. Source URLs, checked dates, duplicate/skipped decisions, and unresolved questions.

For a task-linked report, use the existing `record` helper to save a draft evidence account referencing the report, so the local review desk can preview it and copy prompts for follow-up. Use task category `competitor` for competitor research tasks; the existing parser, registry and inbox accept it. Follow the shared task format and source-write rules when the user requests task creation; do not automatically turn every mention into a new task.

Distinguish completing the research report from carrying out its recommendations. A research task can be complete when its actual research criteria are verified; suggested replies, submissions and follow-ups remain pending until performed and evidenced under their own scope. Report what was researched, saved, and verified, and what remains a draft or unverified lead.

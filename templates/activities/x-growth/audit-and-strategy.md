# Account audit and customized strategy

## Evidence collection

Confirm account identity and access first. Record the audit date, source URLs, coverage window and unavailable fields. Prefer a recent 28-day window and roughly 20–30 original posts plus 10–20 replies when available; these are practical samples, not completeness claims. Include ordinary and weak posts, not just winners. For sparse or new accounts, inspect everything available and state the smaller sample. Never label inaccessible content as absent.

Inspect the profile, header, bio, link destination, pinned post, topic consistency, recent originals, replies and visible conversations. Read linked product facts only for the selected account/product. Choose two or three relevant peer accounts for qualitative comparison when useful: similar audience and resources, plus one aspirational example. Their public counts do not establish conversion rates or the user's potential.

Record each sampled post's URL, publication time, observation time, age, topic, format, opening, proof, CTA, organic/paid/unknown status, and available metrics. Separate originals, replies and reposts. Distinguish public views from analytics impressions; do not silently substitute either. Read actual posts before critiquing them. Public followers and visible reactions cannot reveal profile visits, clicks, audience demographics, follower gains per post or sales.

Capture voice from several actual posts and the user's corrections: typical sentence length, humor, vocabulary, desired directness and topics they avoid. A brand may use a logo and brand name; a person may prefer pseudonymity. Do not require a face, real name, age or location to establish authenticity.

## Diagnose, then prioritize

Use a concise table: area → observation with source → likely implication → specific change → confidence. Mark “not observable” when appropriate. Avoid a precise composite account score without a defensible rubric.

| Area | Useful questions |
|---|---|
| Audience and positioning | Who should follow, for what recurring benefit? Does the content match them? |
| Profile conversion | Do bio, pin and destination explain relevance and demonstrate credible work? |
| Discovery | Are conversations and topics reaching the intended audience? Is distribution too dependent on one channel? |
| Content | Are openings specific, promises delivered, claims supported, and formats suitable for the idea? |
| Relationships | Are there substantive exchanges and returning participants? Are replies relevant to the parent post? |
| Outcome | Is attention producing useful relationships, subscribers, leads, customers or qualified reach? |
| Sustainability | Can this person maintain the routine with their actual time and material? |

Use observable patterns to choose the first experiment:

- Little relevant discovery: improve topic targeting and participate in live audience conversations; retain a modest original-post cadence so profile visitors have something worth following.
- Views but weak profile interest: test clearer audience relevance and a reason to learn more, using available metrics; do not assume views came from qualified people.
- Profile visits but limited follows: test the bio, pin and consistency of the content promise. This is a hypothesis, not proof of a broken profile.
- Audience growth but no desired outcome: inspect fit, offer, destination and CTA before increasing volume.
- Missed tasks: reduce workload and reuse a format before adding tactics.
- Sudden reach decline: check data coverage, age, paid/organic mix, topic changes, account notices and normal variation. Official account-status tools can provide evidence; low impressions alone cannot establish enforcement.

## Plan design

Write a positioning sentence: “For [audience], this account shares [specific recurring value], grounded in [real experience/evidence].” Explain what will change based on this particular audit.

Choose a primary outcome and a few supporting measures. Follower growth can be the primary goal if that is what the user wants; still define which audience would make those followers valuable. Set targets from the account's comparable history and available effort. With no baseline, use a calibration week and a controllable output target rather than extrapolating another creator's sprint.

Content themes should combine the audience's questions with the account owner's real assets: firsthand lessons, worked examples, useful explanations, demonstrations, considered opinions or progress with substance. Use introductions and milestone posts when they serve that audience. Growth updates are not a universal content strategy.

These starting routines are coaching estimates, not X rules or growth guarantees. Count all planned work, including weekly batch writing, against the agreed budget; do not hide an hour of writing outside a “15-minute” routine.

| Capacity | Possible initial allocation |
|---|---|
| 15 minutes on five days | Three days: 10 minutes writing, 5 replying. Two days: 10 minutes conversation discovery, 5 reviewing/logging. Aim for two useful originals per week. |
| 30 minutes on five days | 10 minutes writing, 15 conversation, 5 logging; replace one session with the weekly review. Start with three originals per week. |
| 60 minutes on five days | 20 minutes writing/research, 25 conversation, 15 review and preparation; replace part of one session with weekly analysis. Test a higher cadence only if quality holds. |

Protect focused work and off-days. Being available to respond is useful when feasible, not a requirement to stay online after every post. Start with plausible audience time windows in the user's timezone and test them; there is no universal best hour. Do not recommend payment as the solution to an untested positioning problem.

## Conversation discovery and reply coaching

Create two to four searches using audience vocabulary: a concrete problem, a topical discussion, a relevant peer, or a community introduction. These illustrate syntax, not default topics for every account:

```text
("onboarding" OR "activation") ("struggling" OR "how do you") lang:en
("let's connect" OR "looking to connect") (designer OR accessibility) lang:en -filter:replies
from:example_handle since:2026-10-01
```

Replace examples with the actual niche, language and current dates. Use Latest where available and verify timestamps; search operators and interfaces vary. If an operator produces no usable results, simplify instead of assuming there are no conversations. Optional installed search skills may help; no particular search service is required.

Choose relevance and a real contribution over speed or author size. Read the parent and surrounding discussion. For each selected opportunity save URL, author, timestamp, question/context, why this account can help, proposed contribution and status. Do not reply where the only contribution is praise, a forced question, an unrelated product plug or a request for reciprocal engagement.

A useful reply might contribute a concrete example, ask a necessary diagnostic question, explain a tradeoff or offer a respectful counterexample. The user's actual experience supplies the substance. Never fabricate “I tried this,” a follow action, or a relationship. Do not mechanically add names, questions or follow announcements to every reply.

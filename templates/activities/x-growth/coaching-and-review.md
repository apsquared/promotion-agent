# Coaching and review loop

## Daily or ad hoc check-in

Load the current plan and due tasks. Respond to what the user needs now: choose today's small set of tasks, improve a draft, find a relevant conversation, or remove a blocker. Ask for results only where needed; do not repeat onboarding. Read saved voice feedback before rewriting.

When the user reports completion without a live receipt, label it user-reported. Acknowledge controllable progress such as a clearer argument or a useful conversation; do not invent growth. Record new insights that change the plan, without logging every casual exchange.

For a busy day, select the highest-value action within the available minutes and reschedule the remainder with a reason. For repeated misses, lower the plan's workload. Do not advise catching up with a burst of repetitive posts or replies.

## Draft mentorship

Evaluate audience relevance, opening specificity, delivered value, evidence, voice, clarity, and CTA fit. Give one or two concrete changes rather than a generic score. Show a revised version and explain the most important edit. Offer alternatives only when they test meaningfully different approaches.

Preserve what the author actually knows and believes. Ask for a real example when a claim needs one. Remove unsupported numbers, invented personal experiences, imitation of another creator's phrasing, and fake certainty. A provocative opening still has to deliver a fair promise. A CTA or question is optional when it does not help the reader.

Teach one skill at a time: replace an abstract opening with the specific problem, explain a result using evidence, or write a reply that adds a concrete example. Capture the user's preferences for later drafts. Coaching should gradually improve the user's ability to judge their own work.

## Weekly review

1. Compare the promised work and available time with what actually happened. Distinguish a strategy that was tried from one that was not executed.
2. Compare the current and previous equivalent windows using the definitions in [plan records](plan-records.md). Report data coverage, missing metrics, post ages, paid exposure and any campaign or launch confound. If current data is missing, review execution and content qualitatively without asserting a performance trend.
3. Inspect representative successes and misses in each relevant format. Identify what might explain them and what remains uncertain. A viral exception is a case study, not a new forecast.
4. Decide keep, adapt, stop or collect more evidence for each active experiment. Explain the reason in one or two sentences tied to the original outcome and capacity.
5. Change the smallest useful part of the plan, append the review and reconcile next week's tasks. Give the next action and exact review date in the user's timezone.

Around day 30, revisit the original goal, audience fit, cost in time and money, and the reason to continue. Maintain working routines; retire weak ones. There is no obligation to increase posting frequency each month.

## Experiment card

For each experiment record:

- ID, bottleneck and hypothesis.
- Change, comparison and what will stay similar.
- Eligible posts/conversations and expected effort; primary metric plus a quality/time guardrail.
- Start, review date, evidence source, minimum useful observations and decision rule selected before results.
- Outcome, confounds, confidence, and next action.

Prefer a single meaningful variable. Organic posting is usually an observational comparison, not a randomized A/B test. Match topic, format, post age and paid status where feasible. A few comparable posts can guide a low-cost next test but cannot establish causality. With sparse evidence, extend or simplify rather than declare a winner. Do not optimize for a metric that conflicts with audience quality or the user's business goal.

Example: a consultant whose broad advice gets views but few relevant conversations could test a specific worked example against their usual advice format over two weeks. Judge substantive exchanges with the intended audience and drafting time; track views as context. Decide in advance what would justify repeating it. Do not prescribe a universal numerical win threshold without baseline data.

## Paid growth and account difficulties

Paid promotion is optional. If the user requests it, prepare a bounded proposal with objective, audience, exact creative, maximum budget, duration, measurement window and stop criteria. Obtain applicable spending authorization and verify the current platform offering. Compare organic and paid evidence separately. Follower gains during a campaign are not all campaign-caused; measure retention and intended audience fit if data exists.

If reach drops or an account warning appears, inspect what can be verified and recommend official status/appeal routes where appropriate. Do not suggest changing accounts, disguising automation or adjusting activity just to avoid detection. No diagnosis of hidden enforcement from one low-performing post.

If asked to automate engagement, distinguish supported scheduling of approved original posts from automatic replies or DMs. Check current platform rules and required permissions; a user's request alone does not establish that X has approved a reply bot. Continue with reviewable drafts and human participation when automation is unavailable.

# Launch playbook

Distilled from Product Hunt's [Launch Guide](https://help.producthunt.com/en/collections/19793093-launch-guide) (eight articles, checked 2026-11-09). Re-read the guide when a rule matters to a decision, and update this date.

## How Product Hunt works

- Launches go live at **12:01 AM Pacific** on their scheduled day and run until midnight Pacific. Schedule from tomorrow up to **30 days** ahead; drafts can be saved any time; the day can be changed later. Each person can hunt up to two launches a day.
- Launching is free, including Product of the Day.
- Submitting needs a **personal account** with a verified email. Company and brand accounts can't launch or comment; co-makers use their own personal accounts.
- **Featuring** is an editorial decision. The team reviews every launch and hand-picks the homepage leaderboard; only featured launches compete for the day's ranking and Product of the Day. Meeting the guidelines doesn't guarantee it. A launch that isn't featured is still live, reachable by its link and "See all of today's products".
- **Ranking** uses upvotes, comments, time since launch and other signals; the formula isn't public. A launch with fewer upvotes can rank higher. Upvotes from established, active members count far more than new or low-quality accounts.
- The launch stays on the product's **Product Page**, its permanent home, which collects launches, reviews, awards and followers. Followers are notified of later launches.

## What gets featured

- **Originality**: new in design, experience, audience or approach (not necessarily a new category).
- **A clear edge**: the page says why someone should pick it over the obvious alternative.
- **Plain language**: what it does is clear on first read.
- **It works**: the main flow works in production for a first-time user (clean browser, new phone).
- **New or majorly updated**: a big feature, a new app or a real pivot. A product the community already uses is not news.
- **A product, not content about one**: blog posts, lists and plain documents don't belong. Newsletters and courses need hard-to-find expertise.
- **People can try it**: live, or launching soon with a clear date. A closed beta nobody can join usually isn't a fit.
- **A trustworthy maker.** Launches tied to spam, fake accounts or paid upvotes are removed.

## Myths

- "You need a famous hunter": no. Makers should post their own product; who posts doesn't affect ranking.
- "Linking directly to your product gets you penalized": no, direct links are encouraged.
- "Every upvote counts the same": no.
- "A launch is only worth it at #1": no. Users, feedback and connections are the point.
- "There's a magic weekday": no. Weekdays bring more traffic and more competition; weekends are quieter and can suit small teams and side projects.

## Timeline

Adjust to the time available; a launch scheduled for tomorrow skips to the last rows.

| When | Do |
| --- | --- |
| 4+ weeks before | Set one or two measurable goals. Maker and co-makers create personal accounts with full profiles. Start taking part: comment on launches, answer in the Forums. Fix the first-run flow. List the audience and channels. |
| 2–3 weeks | Pick the day: the team is ready and present, no holiday in the core market, no travel or other release. Check the day's availability. Draft the page copy and the first comment; plan media; decide on an offer. |
| 1–2 weeks | Make the gallery, thumbnail and video or demo. Stage the private draft and read its assessment. Draft every outreach message and the landing page or banner. Set up the pre-launch dashboard: link, badge, Forums thread. |
| 2–5 days | Resolve every blocker. Preview and submit the finished launch (scheduled launches are reviewed; incomplete ones can be removed). Agree the launch-day shifts. Brief co-makers. |
| Launch day | Live at 12:01 AM Pacific. Send outreach in order. Reply to every comment as close to real time as possible; someone is online all day, overnight too for teams outside the US. Track the dashboard. Ask hello@producthunt.com about a sudden drop. Report spam comments. |
| Days after | Keep replying. Follow up with everyone who engaged and remind them of the offer. Measure against the goals and record what worked. Turn feedback into shipped improvements. Share results and thank supporters. Add the award badge if one comes. Ask happy users for reviews. Post updates in the Forums. Advertising is separate from the leaderboard and optional. |

## Launch-day run sheet

Give the user a table with, per time block in their timezone and in Pacific: who is on comment duty, which messages go out, and what to check. Include the launch URL, the first comment's text, answers to likely questions (pricing, privacy, how it differs from the main alternative, roadmap) and a short demo link for replies.

## Already hunted by someone else

Either engage with that launch if it is gaining traction and relaunch later with a major update, or email hello@producthunt.com for help.

## Relaunch

- Relaunch each time you ship a **major** update: a big feature or set of features, a new platform (mobile app, browser extension), a significant redesign or new version, or a pivot. Small fixes, UI tweaks or a new pricing plan go in the Forums instead.
- More than two months since the last launch: submit as usual; it joins the existing Product Page. Under two months: submit a relaunch request explaining what changed; the team reviews it and emails a decision. A link launched in the last two months triggers this check.
- Never change the product URL to get around the check; those launches are removed.
- Focus the tagline, gallery and first comment on the update, credit the community feedback that shaped it, notify past supporters and commenters, and set new goals.
- Founders in Nevo David's playbooks relaunch every few months, each later launch about one feature or use case (Postiz's latest was about its agentic use). Papermark's founder planned every three to four months and said a small feature such as a teams plan is not enough.
